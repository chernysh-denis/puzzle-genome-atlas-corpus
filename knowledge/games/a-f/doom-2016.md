## Analysis scope

- Version / ruleset: current unmodified English Windows Steam application
  `379720`, public Build ID `13954591`, built 2024-04-04, made public
  2024-04-11 and checked 2026-09-04. Only a fresh base single-player Campaign
  on `Hurt Me Plenty` and its complete first mission, `The UAC`, are admitted.
  The current package also contains Update 6.66, Arcade, Photo, multiplayer
  and former multiplayer DLC, but those surfaces do not enter this ruleset.
- Entry: choose Campaign, a fresh save and `Hurt Me Plenty`. The packet begins
  at first direct control after the chained sarcophagus opens. The initial
  armour acquisition, first forced stagger/Glory Kill instruction, pistol and
  combat-shotgun acquisition are part of this first-mission route rather than
  imported progression.
- Primary decision loop: read current objective, local geometry and hostiles,
  health, armour, active weapon, compatible ammunition and transient stagger
  cues; run, strafe, jump and mantle; choose pistol or combat shotgun; aim and
  fire without a reload cycle; when useful, close distance and perform the
  prompted finisher before its opportunity disappears; collect compatible
  combat resources; activate authored doors, the first Gore Nest, the blue
  keycard gate and the final exit console; clear the finite lockdown encounter
  and continue through the fixed Mars route.
- Positive terminal: after the required exterior threats and route gates have
  settled, activate the final console, allow the mission-complete transition
  to finish and retain the `Resource Operations` successor mission. Pausing
  after the Gore Nest or reaching an arbitrary quiet outdoor position is not a
  terminal.
- Negative terminal: health reaching zero ends the current attempt. Reload the
  latest authored Campaign checkpoint; transient position, damage, ammunition,
  pickups, hostile and lockdown state from the failed attempt are replaced by
  the checkpoint state.
- Included: direct first-person traversal; pistol and combat-shotgun selection
  and aimed fire; finite shotgun ammunition and compatible pickups; health and
  armour attrition and contact restoration; enemy stagger, its short-range
  Glory Kill opportunity and resulting health/ammunition drops; authored
  object interactions; the first Gore Nest's finite group and clearance-gated
  lockdown; the blue-keycard route; checkpoint retry; final mission console
  and retained successor access.
- Excluded: secrets, collectibles, Data Logs, exhaustive exploration, score and
  challenge optimisation; Field Drone selection, weapon mods, Elite Guards,
  Praetor upgrades, Runes, Argent Cells and optional Automap completion; frag
  grenade use; the Chainsaw, which the official campaign account introduces in
  the second mission; BFG, later weapons, bosses and every later mission;
  Arcade, Photo, SnapMap, multiplayer, bots, multiplayer progression and DLC;
  alternate difficulties, consoles, ports, mods, cheats, original DOOM games,
  DOOM Eternal, The Dark Ages and the whole franchise or campaign.
- Reproducible parameterisation: use the English Windows public Steam branch,
  default mouse/keyboard inputs, a clean Campaign slot and `Hurt Me Plenty`.
  Follow only the ordinary objective route, take the combat shotgun and blue
  keycard, pull the required Gore Nest organ, defeat its finite released group,
  pass the cleared lockdown and continue to the final exit console. Exact aim,
  weapon choice, damage, pickups, finisher use and completion time are run
  parameters; secrets and optional upgrade objects are ignored.
- Potential scoped modules: `Resource Operations` with its Chainsaw resource
  exchange; one Field Drone weapon-mod choice; one Rune Trial; one Arcade
  score packet; one current multiplayer ruleset. None is imported here.
- Direct-play status: not conducted. Bethesda's official product, campaign,
  Glory Kill, Update 6.66, demo and checkpoint materials establish the product
  and portable systems; the current Steam product and SteamDB public-branch
  record establish lawful Windows availability and observed build; Prima's
  static written route constrains the exact first-mission transitions. This is
  an evidence-backed rules reconstruction, not a claimed captured playthrough
  or local entitlement. No video or audio was opened, played, heard, analysed
  or used.

## Strategic and experiential structure

- Planning horizon: preserve enough health, armour, ammunition and navigable
  space for the next fixed combat pocket rather than spending every strong shot
  on the first visible target.
- Local tactics: keep moving, use the pistol when finite shotgun reserve is not
  worth spending, prioritise visible threats and decide whether closing on a
  staggered hostile is safer than finishing it from range.
- Medium-term structure: the route alternates authored traversal, equipment or
  key interactions, finite combat pressure, resource recovery and a clearance
  gate before its fixed mission exit.
- Failure attribution: visible personal resources, weapon state, local attacks,
  stagger cues and objective markers distinguish poor spacing, target priority,
  ammunition use, missed recovery and route misunderstanding.
- Player-trust factors: the same object interaction releases the bounded group,
  the lockdown visibly clears after finite defeat, checkpoints replace failed
  transient state and the named successor establishes a stable terminal.
- Claim IDs: `DOOM16-005`–`DOOM16-010`.

## Replay and variation

- What changes between sessions: exact aim, movement path, target order,
  resource expenditure, pickup timing and finisher decisions.
- Randomness or procedural generation: the admitted route and encounter gates
  are authored; bounded live combat variation does not make a procedural level.
- Multiple viable strategies: trade safe ranged defeat against riskier
  finisher recovery, and ration shotgun ammunition against pistol fallback.
- Typical replay motive: different difficulty, secrets, challenges and Arcade
  scoring are real replay surfaces but excluded from this first-mission packet.

## Adjacent systems and history

- Direct predecessors: earlier DOOM games share first-person movement and
  weapon combat but are separate products with different rulesets.
- Variants: Arcade and multiplayer package the combat vocabulary inside scoring
  or adversarial rules that this record does not merge.
- Similar games: Serious Sam HD, Half-Life (1998), Half-Life 2, Call of Juarez:
  Gunslinger and other bounded first-person authored routes.
- Important differences: DOOM's short visible stagger window turns close-range
  execution into resource recovery, while its Gore Nest deliberately starts a
  finite lockdown encounter. The first mission has no Chainsaw economy.
