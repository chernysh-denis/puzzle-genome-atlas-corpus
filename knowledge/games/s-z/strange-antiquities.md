# Game: Strange Antiquities

Use the [canonical vocabulary](../../../docs/ARCHITECTURE.md#canonical-vocabulary).
The visitor, artefact names, sense wording and shelf coordinates are instances.

## Analysis scope

- Version / ruleset: original Nintendo Switch digital base game, released
  17 September 2025. Use the English opening request on the first Thursday.
  An English docked Switch 1 recording identifies review build 1.0.12
  and precedes release by two days. A published post-release Switch
  recording shows the same case in French, with its installed patch
  unspecified. No exact binary, entitlement or Switch 2 input parity
  is established.
- Primary decision loop: call Thomas Pope after receiving the reference
  book from Eli White. Read the request and Hunter's Boon entry. Examine
  the loose tooth, compare the physical form and sensory readouts with
  the predator trophy clue, and offer the selected artefact. Acknowledge
  the result and receive the new reference entry. A personal label can
  organise the retained specimen independently.
- Entry: Day 1, Thursday, after Eli's introductory reference handoff,
  with Thomas Pope available as the first requesting customer.
- Exit: Thomas accepts the tooth, the game identifies it as Hunter's
  Boon, and the Selenic Totem entry is received before any work on
  Jack Hungerford's request. The optional label window between those
  customers is included. Final shop closure and the next day lie outside it.
- Included: bell and addressed customer interaction, reference and visual
  evidence, close inspection with independently selected senses, optional
  labels, object-based acceptance and rejection, retained shelf specimen,
  confirmation, and the awarded page.
- Excluded: the demo, other hardware and ports, precise patch parity,
  all other visitor solutions, map travel, the Thaumic Lens and other
  instrument puzzles, exact penalty thresholds and dice recovery, timed
  shopkeeping, and real-world effects or prices.
- Suggested later scope: one request that resolves an instrument reading
  against a different reference and permits two meaningful outcomes.

## Direct-play status

No direct play was performed. Nintendo and Iceberg Interactive establish
the commercial Switch edition. Handheld Players identifies its English
capture as Switch 1 in docked mode, version 1.0.12, with a supplied review
key. Nintendo Town's post-release Switch recording shows the same
handoff sequence in French, including the successor page and later label
entry. An independent English full-game record and written solutions
corroborate the clues and answer, without proving another Switch binary.
Only written sources and static frames were inspected, with no video
or audio playback.

## Mechanical decomposition

### Action Genes

- ACT-341: use the bell to call the current visitor and offer
  the selected object at the addressed counter interaction.
- ACT-752: optionally attach or edit the retained personal label
  independently of the game's identification result.
- ACT-819: enter the object view and select a sensory channel
  without handing over the object.
- Claim IDs: SAQ-002, SAQ-003, SAQ-004, SAQ-005.

### System Behaviour Genes

- SYS-1603: compare the offered specimen against the scripted
  answer. Acceptance names it, retains the artefact, settles the
  request and grants a new page. Rejection leaves the case active
  with its mistake feedback. The retained-offer rule matches the
  botanical exemplar without borrowing grow, value or depletion rules.
- Claim IDs: SAQ-005, SAQ-006.

### Constraint Genes

- CON-821: the current request has a fixed identity predicate.
  A personal label and a similarly themed object cannot substitute
  for the accepted tooth.
- Claim IDs: SAQ-004, SAQ-005.

### Information Genes

- INF-003: the tooth's identity is fixed before the visitor request
  but the player has to uncover its evidence and confirmation.
- INF-576: reference and channel-specific readouts expose different
  pieces of the case before the validated name.
- Claim IDs: SAQ-002–SAQ-005.

### Objective Genes

- OBJ-331: settle Thomas's one request, reach the identified result
  and receive the Selenic Totem reference page.
- Claim IDs: SAQ-005, SAQ-006.

### Time Genes

- TIM-002: read, inspect and commit the offer at chosen discrete moments.
  Deliberation does not consume an observed customer deadline.
- Claim IDs: SAQ-003, SAQ-007.

## Strategic and experiential structure

The name comes from the visitor. The reference describes a predator
trophy without drawing the physical shelf object. Close inspection
lets the player ask different questions about one object before the
offer commits. The acknowledgement is externally visible and adds a
different named page; a self-assigned tag remains distinct.
SAQ-002–SAQ-007.

## Replay and variation

The captures differ in elapsed time, intermediate exploration and optional
label editing. Both show the same tooth accepted and the same successor
entry. The rejected path still has the customer active before the correct
offer. It does not establish a numerical mistake penalty, the exact
recovery sequence on a public Switch build, or every valid action order.

## Adjacent systems and history

The game is a standalone follow-up to Strange Horticulture. The next
customer asks about a container and a drawn symbol, other later cases
use tools, and the day closes on a later event. None is needed to
establish Thomas's terminal result.
