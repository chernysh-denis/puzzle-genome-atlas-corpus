## Decision structure

The player repeatedly commits one complete ordered-symbol proposal against one
already fixed concealed target. The resolver gives exact-position matches
priority, then counts additional shared symbols among the unmatched
occurrences, preventing duplicates from being credited twice. Each proposal and
its two aggregate counts persist as evidence, so the set of consistent targets
can only shrink. The interaction completes when one committed sequence matches
the concealed target at every position.

## Combination match boundary

A hidden answer plus generic “close” feedback is insufficient. The player must
submit a complete ordered sequence; the target must remain fixed; resolution
must partition same-position and additional identity-only matches with
duplicate-safe accounting; and prior query-response pairs must remain usable to
eliminate target candidates. Per-position reveal, unordered subset validation,
one scalar similarity score and a changing target fall outside the boundary.
