## Decision structure

The player reads a finite player-centred projection of the standard
bitruncated `{7,3}` cell graph, then enters one adjacent tile. After that move,
each eligible pursuer automatically advances along a shortest available route.
The useful treasure step is therefore not merely collectible contact: it must
also leave at least one safe continuation after the hostile response.

## Combination match boundary

A curved drawing, ordinary Euclidean hex grid, continuous real-time chase or
turn-based graph without player-centred hyperbolic projection is insufficient.
The player must navigate standard discrete hyperbolic adjacency while automatic
shortest-route pursuit makes preservation of a next legal move decision-bearing.
