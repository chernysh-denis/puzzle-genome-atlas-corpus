## Decision structure

The player assigns one of two diagonal edges to every cell in a finite grid.
Each numbered vertex requires an exact count of incident chosen edges, so one
cell participates in several neighbouring local deductions. At the same time,
all assigned edges form one global graph in which joining two already
connected vertices is forbidden. Completion requires every cell to be assigned
while every local degree and the graph-wide no-cycle predicate hold together.

## Combination match boundary

A visible binary-grid CSP does not instantiate this combination merely because
every cell must be filled. Cell symbols must denote graph edges; displayed
vertex clues must constrain exact incident degree; and the union of all chosen
edges must remain acyclic even at unnumbered vertices. Nonogram has binary
cells and full coverage but constrains ordered runs. Sudoku uses overlapping
units but has neither edge incidence nor global cycle prevention.
