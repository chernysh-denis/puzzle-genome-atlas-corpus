## Decision structure

The player submits one complete fixed-length sequence that must belong to a
recognised lexicon. The system compares it with one unchanged concealed answer,
reserving exact-position occurrences before assigning duplicate-limited
residual matches. The submitted word remains visible with one exact, present
elsewhere or absent / exhausted category at every guess position. Success
requires one complete sequence equal to the answer in all positions.

## Combination match boundary

An arbitrary word puzzle or hidden-code game is insufficient. A complete
ordered sequence must pass a lexicon gate, query one fixed concealed target,
use duplicate-safe exact-first matching, retain position-addressed ternary
feedback and terminate successfully on complete ordered equality. Mastermind
falls outside because arbitrary colour rows are legal and its residual evidence
is aggregated; structured fill-in-the-blank puzzles fall outside because they
assign already visible terms rather than query a hidden answer.
