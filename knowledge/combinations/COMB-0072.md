## Decision structure

The player marks a finite spatial subset. Every selected cell contributes to
one exact horizontal quota and one exact vertical quota, while no two selected
cells may share an edge or corner. The complete selected set must also admit a
perfect one-to-one matching to every fixed target identity through orthogonal
adjacency. Completion requires all three predicates to hold simultaneously;
local adjacency to some target is not enough.

## Combination match boundary

A binary grid puzzle does not instantiate this combination merely because its
rows and columns carry numbers. Those numbers must give exact selected-cell
cardinality, selected cells must exclude their full eight-neighbourhood, and
the completed selection must admit a perfect adjacency-constrained matching to
one fixed target set. Nonogram has ordered run constraints but no target
matching. Slant constrains graph-edge degrees and acyclicity rather than cell
separation and bipartite pairing.
