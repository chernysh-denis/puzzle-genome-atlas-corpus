## Decision structure

The player spends one army's replenishing campaign movement, commits treasury
and turns to a fixed settlement building slot and ends open multi-command
faction turns. Reaching the authored encounter creates a legal pre-battle
deployment interval. The same army is then commanded as live multi-model
formations whose terrain, targets, casualties and morale determine routing.
Victory contracts that battle back into the campaign with persistent survivors,
losses and a completed Rescue state.

## Combination match boundary

An RTS battle, campaign map or turn-based settlement system alone is
insufficient. A supporting ruleset must connect allowance-bound strategic army
movement and prepaid turn-settled construction to spatial deployment and a
manually commanded real-time formation battle, then return morale-sensitive
losses and result to the same persistent campaign objective.

## Novelty and variants

- Age of Empires II: Definitive Edition shares formations, pathing, fog and
  real-time combat, but keeps economy, construction and battle simultaneous on
  one map and does not return a separate battle instance to a campaign token.
- Sid Meier's Civilization VI shares faction turns and city development, but
  resolves combat on the same sequential hex map without deployment or live
  morale routing.
- Hearts of Iron IV shares strategic armies, fog and war progression, but
  combat remains continuous on the theatre map rather than expanding into a
  player-deployed regiment battle.
- Later prologue missions, Realm of Chaos, Immortal Empires, autoresolve and
  multiplayer can support variants only through separate bounded scopes.
