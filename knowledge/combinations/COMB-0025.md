## Decision structure

Agents enter on a schedule and keep walking on a live clock. The player spends
typed finite stock to assign one agent a role; the resulting behaviour then
executes autonomously and may redirect peers, alter terrain or change survival.
Exit and hazard contact account for individual agents differently, and success
requires enough repeated exit rescues rather than control of every member.

## Combination match boundary

A real-time crowd simulation does not instantiate this combination merely
because many units move automatically. The player must assign scarce typed
roles to individual autonomous agents; role execution must continue without
direct steering; agents must enter on a schedule; and fixed terminal zones must
separately account for rescue and loss toward a population quota. Directly
piloted units, one-payload delivery or infrastructure-only editing falls
outside the boundary.
