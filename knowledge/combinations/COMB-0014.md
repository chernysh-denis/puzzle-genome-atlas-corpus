## Combination gene set

- Actions: `ACT-019`
- System Behaviours: `SYS-019`, `SYS-020`
- Constraint: `CON-034`
- Information: `INF-009`
- Objective: `OBJ-011`
- Time: `TIM-005`

This seven-gene set is a proper subset of the supporting fifteen-gene genome.
Fixed board capacity, exclusive occupancy, environmental hazards, emergence,
round horizon, Grid depletion, ordinary relocation and general visible state
remain part of Into the Breach's complete genome but are not required to
identify the intent-disruption interaction.

## Decision structure

The player sees every committed hostile attack, sequences a bounded set of
unit abilities and uses damage or forced displacement to cancel, redirect or
repurpose those attacks. Ending the planning phase hands control to automatic
ordered resolution rather than to another decision-maker.

## Analysed games

- [`GAME-0014` — Into the Breach](../games/g-l/into-the-breach.md)

Fights in Tight Spaces was exhaustively tested and rejected as a complete
supporter: it shares `SYS-019`, `SYS-020`, `INF-009` and `TIM-005`, but replaces

recurring proper subset is recorded as [`COMB-0047`](COMB-0047.md).

Tactical Breach Wizards was also exhaustively rejected: it shares `ACT-019`
and `SYS-020`, but its hostile response is recomputed from the tentative player
draft, movement remains available after an action, and authored room tasks
replace the protected-infrastructure horizon. Its distinct forecast/revision
loop is recorded as [`COMB-0048`](COMB-0048.md).

## Combination match boundary

A tactical game does not instantiate this combination merely because enemies
have turns or display threat ranges. Their concrete attacks must be committed
and disclosed before the player phase, the player must be able to alter their
later outcome through bounded unit actions, and the hostile phase must execute
those commitments automatically.
