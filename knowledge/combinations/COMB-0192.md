## Decision structure

The player converts denars and a settlement offer into a provisioned persistent
party, then repeatedly routes that party into authored contacts. In each live
battle the same player directly moves, rides, attacks and directionally blocks
while issuing formation, movement and attack orders to recruited troops.
Casualties, skill progress and loot return to the campaign party; campaign time
trades food for recovery; the surviving group clears the revealed hideout and
Radagos so the tutorial settles only after clan identity and campaign control.

## Combination match boundary

An open-world melee game, recruit roster, strategy battle or tutorial hideout
alone is insufficient. A supporting ruleset must let one persistent campaign
party buy local recruit bodies and consume provisions, expand contacts into
live battles where the player combines embodied mounted/directional combat with
formation orders, return survivors and loot to that party, and use it to settle
one authored positive terminal beyond the battle result.

## Novelty and variants

- Total War: WARHAMMER III transfers a campaign army through a formation battle
  and back, but the commander is not an embodied directional fighter and the
  prologue uses turn movement/construction rather than recruits and provisions.
- Skyrim Special Edition shares character, equipment, direct combat, skills and
  opening-quest settlement, but its escort is authored rather than a locally
  hired formation receiving battlefield orders.
- XCOM 2 recruits no bodies inside the bounded mission and separates personal
  Action Points into squad turns instead of simultaneous avatar/group command.
- Later Bannerlord field armies, sieges, Sandbox and War Sails may support
  variants only through independently bounded rulesets and terminals.
