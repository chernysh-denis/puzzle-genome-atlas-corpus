## Decision structure

The player navigates one persistent agent to a chosen side of one adjacent
movable object and pushes it from that local access position. Because pulling
is unavailable or insufficient, a legal displacement can preserve ordinary
walking while making completion unreachable. The current geometry and object
state remain visible, so success depends on anticipating access loss rather
than discovering a hidden successor.

## Combination match boundary

Remote piece movement, automatic collision, chain motion without a directly
navigated agent and terminal failure on every bad push do not instantiate the
combination. The game must expose current state, require local navigation to a
push side, move one adjacent object through that contact and admit at least one
non-terminal objective deadlock. Fixed goals, recursive containment, cooking,
terrain growth and ordered stacking are supporting-game specialisations, not
requirements of this shared core.
