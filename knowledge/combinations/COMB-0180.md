## Decision structure

The player commits one National Focus and several research slots while sharing
civilian factories across an ordered state-construction queue and military
factories across persistent equipment lines. Equipment and manpower become
division strength only through reinforcement. Divisions are organised beneath
commanders and persistent geographic plans; their advance remains bounded by
the capital-to-rail-to-hub supply network and supported by eligible regional air
missions. Distributed province and victory-point control finally aggregates
into Ethiopian surrender and concludes the bounded tutorial war.

## Combination match boundary

Generic real-time combat, research or production alone is insufficient. A
supporting ruleset must allocate country-level capacity across parallel
dependencies and equipment lines, translate stockpiles into many fielded units,
retain player-authored fronts, constrain those agents through a multi-stage
supply network and evaluate distributed territorial losses at a country-level
surrender terminal.

## Novelty and variants

- Age of Empires II shares research, unit orders, pathing, vision and live war,
  but production belongs to constructed sites, workers carry resources and
  Conquest tests recoverable units/buildings rather than weighted surrender.
- Civilization VI shares national research, production dependencies and
  territorial strategy, but settles discrete civilization turns and city-local
  projects rather than continuous national factory and front systems.
- DLC focus trees, naval war, diplomacy, occupation and the post-Ethiopia world
  campaign are larger variants outside this combination.
