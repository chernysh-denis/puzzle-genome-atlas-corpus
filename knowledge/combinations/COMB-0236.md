## Decision structure

The player navigates one authored combat chapter, aims and reloads firearms and
operates route fixtures while hostile action continues. A finite special meter
can slow the surrounding action without removing real-time aim, and eligible
combat restores that meter. A separately finite restorative can be consumed
without suspending movement or attack, but its marked recovery settles over
time while later damage remains additive. The selected campaign mode adjusts
eligible hostile performance from recent player effectiveness. Ordered fixture
states must make the final successor threshold traversable before the packet
can close.

## Combination match boundary

Generic firearm combat, a fixed difficulty, an ordinary healing cast or an
isolated slow-time power is insufficient. A supporting ruleset must combine a
finite player-activated tempo modifier, live restorative commitment whose
delayed recovery overlaps later damage, within-mode hostile response to player
effectiveness and ordered authored fixtures leading to a declared successor.

## Novelty and variants

- Call of Juarez: Gunslinger shares direct gunplay, reload, special activation
  and special-meter drain, but its verified interaction centres combo growth,
  duel preparation and narrator-authored state replacement rather than delayed
  medicine or performance-adaptive enemies.
- Grand Theft Auto V shares direct combat, a finite protagonist special,
  firearm handling and authored mission gates, but its healing and opponent
  rules do not supply the same delayed-recovery/adaptive-Fugitive relation.
- Half-Life 2 shares authored route fixtures, weapon handling and checkpointed
  combat, but has neither a player-controlled time-rate resource nor the scoped
  live delayed medicine transition.
