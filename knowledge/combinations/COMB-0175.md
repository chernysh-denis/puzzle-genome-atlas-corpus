## Decision structure

Each player continuously drives one car, routes through reusable boost pads and
uses jump, dodge, aerial orientation and finite thrust to reach one freely
moving ball. Enclosed arena geometry keeps rebounds live. Physical car contact
may bump or temporarily demolish an opponent, but success comes only from
crossing the opposing goal plane. A tie advances through the zero-second ball
state into sudden-death overtime until one goal settles the match.

## Combination match boundary

Vehicle control, football scoring or real-time physics alone are insufficient.
A supporting ruleset must couple a dedicated vehicle's ground-to-air control,
spatially replenished finite boost, one live physical ball, enclosed arena
rebounds, temporary collision removal and goal-only regulation/overtime
settlement.

## Novelty and variants

- EA SPORTS FC 26 shares live ball and goal scoring but has switchable
  footballers, referee restarts and an accepted regulation draw rather than
  enclosed vehicle physics and boost.
- Forza Horizon 6 shares dedicated car control but has ordered race courses,
  Drivatars and event progression instead of one ball and team goals.
- Player count, car hitbox and precise contact values are parameters. Hoops,
  Snow Day, Dropshot, Rumble and mutated boost or goal rules are variants.

## Falsification criterion

Reject support if boost is unlimited or non-spatial by default, the controlled
object is not a vehicle, ball contact is abstract rather than physically live,
ordinary wall contact stops play, demolition is persistent elimination, or a
tied regulation score can remain a final draw.
