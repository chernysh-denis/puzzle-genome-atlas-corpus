## Decision structure

The player repeatedly spends a renewable card budget against previewed hostile
intents while maintaining both persistent player health and a card-commanded
combat proxy. Living proxy health can intercept eligible attack damage and
enables proxy-labelled attacks, so deck selection changes who can absorb and
deal damage. Persistent Quest cards impose an unplayable current draw cost in
exchange for a later route reward, while a one-slot Enchantment commits a
lasting improvement to one card object. Those choices remain inside the same
mutable deck used toward the terminal Act 3 boss.

## Combination match boundary

Ordinary deckbuilding is insufficient. A legal card effect must directly
depend on a separate proxy's alive/dead combat state, that proxy must absorb a
bounded class of otherwise incoming damage, and the persistent deck must admit
both a delayed unplayable Quest contract and a single-slot permanent card
modifier during the same terminal-health climb.

The original Slay the Spire supports the broader `COMB-0119` but lacks Osty,
Quest-card contracts and the admitted one-Enchantment-per-card boundary.
Inscryption's creatures occupy lanes as independent combatants rather than one
proxy health state commanded by ordinary effect cards, so it does not support
this complete subset.
