## Decision structure

The player grows typed mana access by playing a land, then chooses whether to
cast, activate or preserve a response. Each committed spell or non-mana ability
enters a LIFO stack; both players can add a newer object or pass before only the
top resolves. Public permanents then form an attacking subset and a legal
blocker relation, while concealed hands and libraries keep responses uncertain.
Combat, card text and state-based checks eventually create the first player
loss and settle the single game.

## Combination match boundary

Generic card draw or turn-based combat is insufficient. A supporting ruleset
must grow typed cast capacity through turn-limited resource sources, give both
players nested response priority, resolve a LIFO rule-object stack, construct
attacker and blocker relations from persistent creatures and settle one of
several rules-level player-loss predicates.

## Novelty and variants

- Slay the Spire uses a private flexible card phase followed by autonomous
  hostile intent, with no opponent response priority, blocker declaration or
  stack.
- Inscryption uses fixed paired lanes and a relative damage scale rather than
  land-based typed mana, free blocking relations and life/library terminals.
- Other Arena formats, deck lists and card pools may support the same kernel,
  but deck construction, sideboarding and digital-only mechanics are separate
  scope modules.
