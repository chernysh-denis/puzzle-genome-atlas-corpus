## Combination gene set

`ACT-229 + ACT-290 + ACT-309 + SYS-320 + SYS-368 + SYS-515 + SYS-691 + SYS-729 + CON-282 + INF-205 + OBJ-142 + TIM-003`

This is a strict twelve-gene subset of the sixteen-gene Need for Speed: Most
Wanted (2005) genome. Automatic-control commitment, purchase authority, generic
driving guidance and the resource gauges remain outside the distinctive
race-to-loss-to-replacement interaction.

## Decision structure

The player directly drives against one rival while deciding when to spend
ordinary nitrous and when to enter or leave the temporary Speedbreaker state.
Live line and rival decisions continue until an authored vehicle failure
overrides ordinary finish evaluation. The packet then removes the assigned
car and closes only after the fixed replacement path reaches retained campaign
control.

## Combination match boundary

Generic racing, temporary boost or a cutscene loss alone is insufficient. A
supporting ruleset must combine direct live rival driving, an ordinary finite
acceleration reserve, a separate draining/recharging temporary focus modifier,
an unavoidable authored vehicle-loss override and a replacement-asset
checkpoint beyond that loss.

## Novelty and variants

- Need for Speed Underground shares direct rival racing but requires a normal
  first-place finish and retained Bank rather than a forced loss.
- Need for Speed Unbound has technique-earned Burst Nitrous and a post-race
  pursuit before banking, but its scoped event still classifies a finish.
- Need for Speed Payback has ordinary nitrous and authored driving transitions,
  yet the Highway Heist is won by delivery and does not remove the initial car
  as a forced prologue reset.
- Grand Theft Auto V exposes a protagonist-specific driving-focus meter, but
  its campaign does not bind that meter to this loss-and-replacement terminal.
