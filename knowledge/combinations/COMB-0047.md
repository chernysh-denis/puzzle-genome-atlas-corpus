# COMB-0047 — Previewed hostile intent redirected before ordered resolution

- Claim status: `Strong Pattern`
- Evidence quality: `Corroborated`
- Confidence: `High`
- Corpus role: recurring interaction
- Novelty assessment: derivative recurrence verified; independent-family
  recurrence remains open because the publisher explicitly names Into the
  Breach as a positional design source.

## Combination gene set

- System Behaviours: `SYS-019`, `SYS-020`
- Information: `INF-009`
- Time: `TIM-005`

This four-gene set is a proper subset of both supporting genomes and of
`COMB-0014`. It isolates the recurring causal loop without requiring Into the
Breach's mech command grammar or Grid objective. Recurrence does not imply
independent invention.

## Decision structure

Before the player phase, hostile attacks are committed and expose their actors,
targets, effects and order. The player may use displacement to change which
body or space will receive an attack, then ends the phase and watches surviving
hostiles execute those commitments automatically. A hostile attack is therefore
both a threat and a temporarily redirectable board resource.

Into the Breach supplies persistent mech abilities and a per-mech move/action
allowance. Fights in Tight Spaces supplies one agent's random card hand and a
shared momentum pool. Those action economies are deliberately outside the
combination because neither is required for the shared intent-redirection loop.

## Combination match boundary

A tactics game does not instantiate this combination merely by displaying
threat ranges or predicting a likely response. Every matched hostile attack
must already be committed before planning, disclose target / effect / relative
order, remain mechanically redirectable through displacement and then execute
automatically unless an explicit cancellation condition intervenes.

Reactive forecasts that recompute an enemy response from tentative player
actions do not satisfy `INF-009`. Displacement with no committed attack, or an
enemy phase that chooses fresh actions after the player commits, also fails.

Tactical Breach Wizards is the canonical counterexample for the reactive-
forecast exclusion. It shares `SYS-020`, but its target state may change during
the player's draft and Foresee simulates a reversible consequence before
commit; it therefore supports [`COMB-0048`](COMB-0048.md), not this record.

## Relation to COMB-0014

`COMB-0047` is intentionally nested inside
[`COMB-0014`](COMB-0014.md). The larger seven-gene record additionally requires
`ACT-019`, `CON-034` and `OBJ-011`: persistent unit-ability targeting, per-unit
move-then-ability allowance and protected shared infrastructure through a
bounded horizon. Fights in Tight Spaces lacks all three, so the smaller record
captures derivative recurrence without rewriting the original boundary.
