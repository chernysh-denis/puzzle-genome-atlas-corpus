## Decision structure

The player launches one stationary designated body on a chosen straight
heading. The system carries it through intermediate cells until a stop surface
or forward obstruction ends travel, collecting every required token crossed on
the way. Because touching any visible hazard during that indivisible path ends
the attempt, each input selects a complete safe ray rather than one adjacent
step. Repeating safe launches must clear every declared target.

## Combination match boundary

A game does not instantiate this combination merely because an object slides,
collectibles exist or mines are visible. Input must commit one designated body
to automatic unchanged-heading traversal; intermediate contact must acquire
required board targets without stopping; an in-path visible hazard must be
terminal; and each launch must resolve completely before the next. Golf Peaks
uses finite trajectory cards and a destination hole but does not collect
distributed targets or route around terminal in-path mines. Snakebird collects
targets but retains one-cell head control and activates a separate exit.
