## Decision structure

Two survivors divide partial exploration and recurrent harvesting, return
materials to a shared site and convert them into fires, food, science access and
persistent fixtures. Live time simultaneously consumes hunger, fuel and food
freshness, changes sanity and daylight and carries the world from Autumn into
Winter. Individual death becomes a ghost whom only the living partner can
restore with a paid Telltale Heart; collective death can reset the world. The
same coupled economy must keep both survivors embodied at a warm viable base
until the first Spring boundary.

## Combination match boundary

Generic real-time crafting or co-op combat is insufficient. A supporting
ruleset must connect renewable spatial harvesting to station-gated personal
science, placeable survival fixtures, perishable food, depleting local
light/heat, a mental-state danger system, a shared seasonal clock, asymmetric
living-to-ghost recovery and a bounded cooperative survival terminal.

## Novelty and variants

- Minecraft shares embodied gathering, crafting, inventory and survival but
  does not make sanity thresholds materialise attackers or require one living
  partner to restore a ghost.
- Terraria shares station crafting, day-night ecology and survival structures
  but does not retain prototype knowledge per survivor or bind the packet to a
  default first-Winter climate cycle.
- Rust shares a generated survival world, crafting and metabolism but its
  ordinary death/respawn and player-territory economy do not reproduce this
  cooperative ghost-and-season kernel.
- Other DST modes, Caves, later seasons, characters and event settings may
  support variants, but they remain separate scope modules.
