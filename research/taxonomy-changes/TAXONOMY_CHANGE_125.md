# Taxonomy Change 125: first-world collection and two distinct lair gates

## Status

- Proposal status: Accepted
- Claim status: Observation
- Evidence quality: Corroborated
- Confidence: High
- Date: 2026-09-24
- Trigger: `GAME-0386` Banjo-Kazooie, original North American Nintendo 64
  first visit to Mumbo's Mountain through opening Treasure Trove Cove.
- Scope: five additive Active genes; no older signature, lifecycle, family
  definition or verified combination changes.

## Problem and accepted change

The N64 first-world route crosses three different collection boundaries that
must not be flattened into a generic pickup counter. It pays five Mumbo
tokens for a temporary geometry-changing termite body, retains a world-best
Note tally while individual Notes respawn on interruption, and fills an
addressed world picture with Jiggies after a separate non-spending Note-door
threshold. Five distinct rescued Jinjos jointly emit one Jiggy.

- `SYS-1042`: authored token payment creates a temporary alternate
  traversable body; it is not a picked-up Mario power-up or permanently learned
  move.
- `SYS-1043`: world-best Note score persists while individual Note objects
  reset, specifically in the original N64 ruleset.
- `SYS-1044`: a complete spatial rescue set emits one fixed reward, unlike
  typed inventory donations or repeated scalar pickup thresholds.
- `CON-691`: collected pieces must be placed into the addressed picture;
  merely having two pieces or passing the 50-Note Door is insufficient.
- `OBJ-228`: the bounded study route ends at the reachable next-world
  entrance after collection controls and both lair gates, not at campaign end.

## Transfer and rejection test

`SYS-398` retains Bottles-taught moves and `CON-349` makes their use legal on
compatible terrain. `CON-440` covers the Note Door because the best Note
total is checked without being spent. `SYS-902` applies a temporary platform
state from a pickup, but Mumbo's token-priced fixture replaces the body with
different traversal geometry. `SYS-608` describes a temporary *combat* body
with its own health, which the termite is not. `SYS-116` consumes typed
inventory contributions into slots, while rescuing Jinjos marks dispersed
world members. `OBJ-048` requires exact-cover shape fitting; the Jiggy
picture accepts generic pieces one slot at a time. No older game is silently
assigned the new distinctions.

- [Original Nintendo 64 instruction booklet

- [Original-N64 first-world written
  route](https://gamefaqs.gamespot.com/n64/196694-banjo-kazooie/faqs/29761)
  places the fifth token inside the termite mound before transformation and
  carries the 100-Note/ten-Jiggy visit into the next world's two-piece picture.
- [Independent N64 Note-door
  guide](https://gamefaqs.gamespot.com/n64/196694-banjo-kazooie/faqs/3143)
  gives the first door's 50-Note threshold.
- No cartridge or audiovisual direct-play trace was inspected. The study's
  full first-world controls are stricter than the game's minimum 50-Note and
  two-Jiggy successor admission rule.
