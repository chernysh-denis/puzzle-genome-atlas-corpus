- Scope: wording and supporting evidence for `ACT-093`, `ACT-148`, `ACT-341`,

## Current classification

Five lower-ID boundaries described transferable mechanisms through their first
carrier's narrower product form. Stardew Valley supplied irreversible typed
quantities only to collection slots; Oxygen Not Included placed plans for
autonomous workers; Palworld combined reserved materials with player or
companion work; the contextual-object Action named authored objects; and
Resident Evil 2 made nearby hostile absence mandatory at its save fixture.

The Forest independently separates the same responsibilities. The player
places and rotates a known Temporary Shelter outline, contributes compatible
sticks and leaves one unit at a time, and receives the usable shelter only when
every displayed requirement is filled. The completed player-created object
then exposes a contextual save command. Its fixture predicate is completion and
reach, not Resident Evil's nearby-hostile rule, while sleeping is a separate
command that destroys this one-use shelter.

## Complete lower-ID and transfer scan

Every lower-ID Active Action, System and Constraint definition was searched,
then every carrier of the five selected owners was reread.

- `ACT-338` begins from an already crafted deployable fixture and commits the
  finished item directly, so it excludes a persistent unsupplied outline.
- `ACT-204` immediately spends material at a connected block or socket; it
  does not create a freely positioned plan with separately filled quantities.
- `ACT-123` selects a personal recipe output and does not own world-plan
  placement or the later per-requirement contributions.
- `SYS-116` completes a named collection group and grants its authored reward;
  a construction outline instead becomes the structure described by its plan.
- `SYS-162`, `SYS-235` and `SYS-550` require autonomous construction workers;
  The Forest's survivor performs every admitted contribution directly.
- `CON-602` requires a live exposed interaction interval and fixture cooldown;
  neither is evidenced for the Temporary Shelter save.
- `CON-629` follows campaign context rather than proximity to one physical
  fixture. It does not cover a shelter-specific local save gate.

The first and new carriers vary collection versus construction receiver,
authored versus player-created fixture, supply timing, contributor, and local
save predicate. They retain the same action or settlement responsibility in
each selected owner, so the transfer test passes without a new synonymous gene.

## Decision

- Generalise `ACT-093` from a collection slot to any visible item requirement
  with an irreversible typed-quantity transfer.
- Generalise `ACT-148` from autonomous-worker plans to a persistent material-
  backed plan completed by a separate player or worker step.
- Generalise `SYS-312` so accepted material supply and optional work may settle
  at their carrier-declared stages before the completed output appears.
- Generalise `ACT-341` from authored objects to authored or player-created
  stateful world objects while retaining its contextual interaction boundary.
- Generalise `CON-621` from Resident Evil's mandatory hostile predicate to an
  eligible designated world fixture with carrier-local predicates and no
  required exposure interval or cooldown.
- Generalise the `FAM-013` description from authored recipients and fixtures
  to addressed recipients and fixtures, so the family admits both authored
  and player-created construction plans without changing its identity.
- Add `OBJ-171` separately. Construction plus accepted fixture save is the
  bounded terminal, not a parameter of any earlier Objective.

## Rejection condition

Reopen this generalisation if a later carrier shows that contribution to a
construction requirement is reversible, that a plan and completed object are
not distinct states, or that a purported fixture save is actually a free menu
command or a live cooldown-bearing interaction. Different material counts,
worker participation, consumption timing, fixture origin or local eligibility
predicate alone is insufficient.
