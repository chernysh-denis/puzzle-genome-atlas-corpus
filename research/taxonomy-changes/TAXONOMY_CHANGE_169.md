## Problem and accepted change

One binary advisor response can shift several competing reserves; church, people, army and treasury each kill a monarch at either extreme. The next card is not sampled from a uniform fixed deck: current kingdom state and recent appearances filter the pool, then unequal weights govern selection. A fallen monarch is replaced by an heir whose ordinary power bars reset while selected authored narrative availability may persist. Present bars and tilt-time affected-domain dots inform a choice without predicting signed effects. These are causally distinct from merely swiping, random sampling or restarting a conventional failed run.

- Reject `INF-001`: visible bars and unsigned affected-domain cues do not reveal the hidden story conditions, future card or signed outcome.
- Do not invent a fixed first-card sequence, numeric weights, signed preview or guaranteed named unlock after the first death. Later anniversary content is outside the original-release scope.

## Evidence and limits

[François Alliot's 2016 developer account](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/game-design-deep-dive-creating-an-adaptive-narrative-in-i-reigns-i-) directly describes binary advisor cards, four dimensions, state/recency filtering, unequal weights and some permanent deck additions. [Devolver's App Store description](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/reigns/id1114127463) describes the original mobile swipe/balancing/dynasty loop but now also advertises later updates, which are excluded. [Emily Short's contemporary firsthand analysis](https://emshort.blog/2016/08/20/reigns/) establishes both-end deaths and heir reserve reset; its dated discussion describes an unsigned affected-domain cue. No original iOS binary, direct input trace or audiovisual evidence was inspected, and the exact tilt presentation on iOS remains lower-confidence than the core rules.

## Decision

Accept six new boundaries with reviewed Ukrainian wording, complete lower-ID comparison, original mechanically coherent artwork and repository/browser gates. This last game unit authorises one local commit only; no push, public corpus publication or deployment.
